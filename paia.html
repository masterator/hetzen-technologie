Prepared under section 51 of the Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000 (PAIA) · Compiled 25 November 2026 · Next review November 2027

What this is

PAIA gives everyone the right to ask a private business for records it holds, where needed to protect a right. This manual explains which records Hetzen Technologies keeps and how to ask for them. To simply see or correct your own personal information, you don't need this process; just ask us (see our Privacy Policy).

6. How we process personal information (POPIA)

Why: to answer enquiries, quote, deliver and support projects, invoice, keep legal records and keep systems secure.

Whose information: customers and potential customers (people and businesses), their contact people, and suppliers.

Who receives it: service providers that run our systems (Supabase, GitHub, Google, Meta/WhatsApp, Cloudflare), services set up on a customer's behalf, our accountant, and authorities where the law requires.

Sent outside South Africa: yes. Our business records are hosted by Supabase in Ireland (EU), and some providers operate worldwide, under protections permitted by section 72 of POPIA.

Security: two-step sign-in, owner-only database rules, encrypted connections, least-access accounts and regular security checks. See our Security page.

7. How to request a record

  1. Complete PAIA Form 2 (Request for Access to Record), which you can get from the Information Regulator's website.
  2. Send it to our Information Officer by email.
  3. Say which record you want, how you'd like to receive it, and which right you need it to protect.
  4. We'll reply within 30 days. We may extend this once by up to 30 more days if the law allows, and we'll tell you why.

Fees: the request and access fees set in the PAIA Regulations may apply. We'll tell you the exact amount before you pay. No fee applies when you ask for your own personal information.

Refusals: we may refuse access only on the grounds in Chapter 4 of Part 3 of PAIA, such as protecting someone else's privacy or confidential commercial information. We'll give you reasons in writing.

If you're unhappy: you may complain to the Information Regulator at PAIAComplaints@inforegulator.org.za (PAIA Form 5), or apply to court.
